# Research Report: Alternatives to LSTM for EV Demand Forecasting

You asked an excellent strategic question: *"If we replace LSTM, what are our other options that will not break our research novelty?"*

Your current novelty is: **"Monte Carlo to AI Cold-Start Forecasting"** (training a neural network on synthetic data to predict the future). 
The good news is that replacing LSTM with a newer architecture will not break this novelty—in fact, choosing the right alternative will **upgrade and increase your novelty**, because LSTM is considered a slightly older architecture in 2026.

Here are the top three state-of-the-art alternatives that would make your PhD methodology even stronger.

---

### Option 1: Time-Series Transformers (The Best Choice for Maximum Novelty)
Transformers (the same underlying math that powers ChatGPT) have recently taken over time-series forecasting. Unlike LSTMs which look at data sequentially (step-by-step), Transformers use "Self-Attention" to look at the entire 24-hour curve at once.
*   **Why it's better:** They are incredibly good at finding long-range, complex hidden patterns. They also train much faster than LSTMs because they process data in parallel.
*   **Impact on Novelty:** **Massive Upgrade.** Using a Transformer (like *Autoformer* or *Informer*) for EV demand forecasting is currently the bleeding-edge of academic research. 
*   **New Novelty Title:** *Monte Carlo to Transformer Cold-Start Forecasting.*

### Option 2: TCN (Temporal Convolutional Networks)
TCNs use the same math used for image recognition (CNNs) but applied to a 1D time-series. 
*   **Why it's better:** EV charging demand is very "spiky" (sharp peaks and sudden drops). TCNs are mathematically superior to LSTMs at capturing rapid, sharp local fluctuations. They are also much easier and faster to train on your laptop's Intel i3 processor.
*   **Impact on Novelty:** **Strong Upgrade.** TCNs are highly respected in smart-grid research for their stability and speed compared to LSTMs.

### Option 3: N-BEATS (Neural Basis Expansion Analysis)
N-BEATS is a pure deep learning architecture built specifically by AI researchers for time-series forecasting. 
*   **Why it's better:** Deep learning is usually a "black box" (you don't know why it made the prediction). N-BEATS is unique because it is *interpretable*. It splits its forecast into two visual outputs: the "Trend" and the "Seasonality". 
*   **Impact on Novelty:** **High Upgrade.** Examiners love N-BEATS because you can actually look inside the AI's math and explain *why* it predicted the 2035 curve to look a certain way.

### Option 4: GRU (Gated Recurrent Unit)
*   **Why it's better:** GRU is the "younger brother" of LSTM. It works almost exactly the same way, but it has fewer mathematical gates. This means it uses less RAM and trains faster on lower-end laptops.
*   **Impact on Novelty:** **Neutral.** It is scientifically identical in novelty to an LSTM. It is just a more efficient version of the exact same concept.

---

### My Recommendation
If you want to move away from LSTM but keep the highest possible academic novelty for your PhD, I highly recommend switching to **Option 1: Transformers** or **Option 2: TCN**. 

*   If you want the most "buzzword" heavy, state-of-the-art AI: Choose **Transformers**.
*   If you want the model that is fastest to run on your specific Intel i3 laptop while still being highly novel: Choose **TCN**.
